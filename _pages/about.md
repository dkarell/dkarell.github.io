I am an Assistant Professor of Sociology at Yale University. I teach courses on integrating AI into social science research methods, computational approaches to studying culture, and the sociology of backlash. I also co-organize Yale's [Computational Social Science Workshop](https://isps.yale.edu/computational-social-science-workshop).

My current research uses computational and quantitative methods to examine how technology affects culture and politics. For example, recent projects investigate the impact of [generative AI on sociological practice and society](https://osf.io/preprints/socarxiv/wb72h_v1), the role of [social media in civil unrest](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/00031224231156190), the effect of [prediction markets on political speech](https://osf.io/preprints/socarxiv/n6zxw_v2), and the influence of [data center backlash on partisan identities and behavior](https://osf.io/preprints/socarxiv/sfvtr_v1). This last project extends earlier work on the social and cultural dynamics of backlash, which focused on the Blue Lives Matter movement. For more information about my research, see [here](https://dkarell.github.io/research/).
